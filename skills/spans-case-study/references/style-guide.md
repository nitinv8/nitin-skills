# Spans case study style guide

Derived from a review of spans.co.in/case-studies and the Britannia, Lotte India, KRBL, Disha Foods and Granules studies, plus the index page.

## Page anatomy (in order)
1. Breadcrumb: Home / Case Studies / {Client}
2. Header: client name, `Industry: ... Location: ...`
3. **The Challenge**: headline, 2 paragraphs (150-250 words total), **Key Issues** list (3-5, each with a warning marker)
4. **The Solution**: headline, 2-3 paragraphs, **Technologies Deployed** chips (4-8), **Implementation Timeline** (3-4 numbered phases)
5. **The Results** (or **Engineering & Design Highlights**): headline plus 4-6 tiles (big value, label, one-line note)
6. Client quote with name and designation (when available)
7. CTA: "Achieve Similar Results for Your Facility", Contact Us

Index card: tech tag, client, `Industry - Sub-sector - Location`, "The Challenge" one-liner, 3 metrics, "Read Full Case Study". Cards are grouped by four categories: Food & Beverage; Pharmaceutical & Healthcare; Chemicals, Industrial, Power & Metals; Municipal, Infrastructure & Water.

## Voice
- Confident, engineering-led, plain English. Spans is "we" or "Spans Envirotech". Client is the protagonist.
- Recurring themes (use when true): designed for **actual operating conditions** not theoretical assumptions; **total cost of ownership** over initial capex; **retrofit and re-use** of existing civil assets; **zero downtime** execution; **reuse-ready** and **ZLD-ready** designs; simplified O&M and automation.
- Headlines state the outcome or transformation: "Trickling Filter Converted to High-Performance Two-Stage MBBR System", "Re-Purposing Existing Structures with an MBBR Conversion", "A Value-Engineered Aerobic Scheme After a Full Technology Trade-Off Study".
- Open the Challenge with the client's credentials in one sentence, then the trigger event. Close the Solution paragraph with why the approach fits the client's constraint.
- Avoid: superlatives without evidence ("world-class", "best-in-class"), vendor brand names, jargon without a gloss on first use (MBBR, DAF, SMAR, UF, ZLD), and naming past contractors.

## Numbers
- Prefer before to after pairs with units: `COD ~6,000 mg/L to <250 mg/L`, `125 to 300 KLD`.
- Use one flow unit per study (KLD or m³/day), matching the source documents.
- Rupees as `₹85 Lakhs`, `₹2 Cr`. Compare like with like (cost of retrofit vs cost of new build).
- Include design parameters in the Solution text when they show engineering depth (MLSS 6,000-8,000 mg/L, aeration 1,100-1,200 m³/hr).
- Cite the standard met by name (EPA 1986 Schedule VI, CPHEEO, UKPCB, Dubai Municipality).

## Delivered vs design studies
| | Delivered | Design / engineering | Audit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Section title | The Results | Engineering & Design Highlights | Findings & Recommendations |
| Verbs | reduced, achieved, saved | designed for, targets, sized for | identified, quantified, evaluated |
| Tile labels | COD Removal | COD Design Target | Audit Coverage, Options Evaluated |
| Quote | Optional | Usually none | Optional |

## Strong and weak patterns seen in the published set
Strong:
- **KRBL**: concrete baseline (650 m³/day, BOD 1,300, COD 3,000), explicit constraints, design parameters, phased execution, measured results, investment versus new-build comparison, specific quote.
- **Lotte**: mismatch between design assumptions and real load (200 KLD plant treating only 125 KLD at COD ~6,000) is the story hook; results tie to the original problem.
- **Disha / Granules**: honest design-stage labelling, scope split between Spans and client, trade-off study narrative.

Weak, do not repeat:
- **Britannia**: no flows, influent values, location or technology names; results are qualitative ("Reduced Operational Burden"). Ask for specifics before writing a similar story.
- **Index cards that disagree with detail pages** (Britannia, KRBL cards cite metrics absent from the pages). Card metrics must be copied from result tiles.
- Mixed spelling (optimisation vs equalization). Use British spelling throughout.

## Image and diagram asks
Always request: a process flow diagram of the final train, one plant photo (client approval), and where a retrofit is central, a before/after photo or layout. Suggest alt text and a caption for each.
